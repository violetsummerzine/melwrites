Context

What Violet Verse is

Violet Verse is decentralized software for building websites for tokenized communities that want to showcase and/or monetize their information using cryptocurrency. It was originally built with JavaScript / React Native, with wallet capabilities including FLOW and Phantom, a Privy login to capture wallet addresses, and a Vercel virtual environment to log in to the backend.

VV was built as a modern version of WordPress, using a newer codebase that allows for modularity to help writers get compensated with crypto. A decentralized model allows the software to send native tokens to anyone who signs up on the platform to read, contribute, or tip the writer, as each user has a unique profile.

Product

What I built

As founder, I led the product from the first concept to launch. The goal was to create a seamless login process using Web3 wallet infrastructure, and a high-fidelity blogging platform with integrated tipping capabilities, built-in formatting, and easy uploads on each page. We deliberately left out typography selection to standardize how content is presented in live mode. The first users were early-adopter crypto degens and airdrop hunters.

Learned

What it taught me

Building Violet Verse taught me:

  • How technical products can be stalled due to blockchain updates and out-of-date plugins. At the last minute, the login partner had to be switched to another provider due to capability issues. Since launch, VV’s wallet provider has been changed twice.
  • At launch, our initial users were incentivized by a native token, so we attracted a global audience and the content was very Web3-friendly. Now, our users are more tech-curious and not as interested in the crypto layer.
  • Leading a product end-to-end taught me to focus on where the initial engagement is coming from with minimal marketing and strategy efforts, research why, and then try to scale it by identifying niche communities that already have a strong presence.
  • This experience has shaped how I approach product marketing: planning launches that actually make sense for the technical product’s lifecycle, messaging to technical and consumer audiences in a way that helps drive product adoption, and understanding why products become successful.
