Context

The brief

Start with the situation. Who was the client, what did they need, and why did it matter? One or two short paragraphs that a stranger could follow.

Add the constraint that made it interesting — a tight timeline, a new audience, a tricky subject — so the reader understands the stakes.

Approach

Building the system

Walk through how you approached the work: research, interviews, drafts, the moment the idea clicked. Show your thinking, not just the output.

Name the decisions you made and why. Detail is what makes a case study persuasive.

Outcome

What changed

Close with what happened. Numbers help, but so does a sentence about how the work was received or what it changed for the people who used it.
